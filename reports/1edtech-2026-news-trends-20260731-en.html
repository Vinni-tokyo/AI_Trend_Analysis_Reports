Top 5 Trends

1. Major Restructuring: The Dual 1EdTech Global + Consortium Model

Current status: During the week of June 2, 2026, 1EdTech officially announced its reorganization into two coordinated entities: 1EdTech Global Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to public-good work, and 1EdTech Consortium Inc., which continues the traditional membership-based standards development. CEO Curtiss Barnes championed the restructuring after twelve months of preparation, explaining the rationale: "The field has been trying to solve a coordination problem with tools designed for competition."

Core of the New Structure

• 1EdTech Global: A 501(c)(3) nonprofit responsible for public-infrastructure activities
• 1EdTech Consortium: Continues as the membership-based standards development organization
• Learn & Work Ecosystem Library joins: The library, with 2,255+ curated resources on digital credentials and workforce innovation, agreed in principle to join 1EdTech Global while maintaining editorial independence

Background and Significance

• The public-infrastructure problem: Edtech standards are essential public infrastructure yet have lacked sustainable long-term funding
• Funding diversification: Moving beyond membership fees to philanthropy, partnerships, and mission-aligned services
• Long-term stability: The goal is stable, long-term infrastructure support for the entire edtech ecosystem

Weaknesses: Actual results of funding diversification remain to be verified over time

Outlook: This restructuring signals the evolution of a standards body from a "membership consortium" into a "public infrastructure steward." As collaboration with foundations and governments grows, non-member institutions are likely to benefit more easily from the standards ecosystem.

2. Interoperability Moves from Optional to Required: The French Decree and 1EdTech Europe

Current status: The first trend 1EdTech highlights for 2026 is interoperability shifting "from best practice to mandatory requirement." France adopted the Interoperability Framework for Digital Services for Education, a decree requiring digital tools used in public middle and high schools to comply with national requirements for security, interoperability, and responsible digital technology. The framework explicitly names 1EdTech standards: OneRoster (class and enrollment data), LTI (tool-to-platform connections), and Common Cartridge and QTI (sharing learning content and assessments).

France's Interoperability Decree

• Scope: All digital tools and services used by public middle and high schools
• Named standards: OneRoster, LTI, Common Cartridge, QTI and other internationally recognized open standards
• Market effect: Lower technical barriers ease market entry for open tools, fostering a more open, competitive, and sustainable edtech ecosystem

Launch of 1EdTech Europe

• Co-founded: KIC (Knowledge Innovation Centre) and 1EdTech launched an independent nonprofit shaping open edtech standards for European learners, institutions, and providers
• Key mission: Developing European profiles of LTI and Edu-API adapted to European institutional, legal, and privacy frameworks
• Event: Learning Impact Europe 2026 is scheduled for September

📊 Credibility Assessment: 8.5/10
Strengths: Cross-verified across 1EdTech articles, press releases, and KIC's announcement
Weaknesses: Details such as enforcement timeline and penalties require consultation of the original decree
🔗 Related Links: 1EdTech Article on the French Decree | KIC: 1EdTech Europe | LTI

Outlook: 1EdTech predicts more governments and institutions will follow France's approach. For edtech vendors, standards certification is becoming a bidding qualification for public procurement rather than a marketing point.

3. AI Moves from Experimentation to Governance: The Expanded TrustEd Apps Generative AI Rubric

Current status: 1EdTech assesses that in 2026 AI is advancing "beyond experimentation into policy and governance." The critical challenge is defining boundaries: which educational data and context can be safely shared with AI systems, and what must remain protected. To support this, an expanded version of the TrustEd Apps Generative AI Data Rubric is expected in early 2026, providing a foundation for the ethical, productive, and safe use of generative AI data in education.

TrustEd Apps Program Components

• Pledge & certification: TrustEd Apps Pledge plus data privacy and interoperability vetting and certification
• Rubrics: Self-assessment criteria, including the Generative AI Data Rubric
• TAMS & Directory: A management suite that streamlines tool evaluation, and a public directory of certified products

Direction of AI Governance

• From tools to ecosystems: "Success won't come from adopting more tools, but from building ecosystems grounded in interoperability, governance, and shared responsibility"
• Matchmaking: TAMS connects institutions with vetted AI tools, enabling innovation with less risk
• Academic uptake: Related research frameworks such as TEAS (Trusted Educational AI Standard) are emerging

4. Digital Credentials: Open Badges 3.0 and CLR 2.0 Enter Mass Adoption

Current status: 1EdTech's Open Badges 3.0 and CLR (Comprehensive Learner Record) 2.0 have been updated for alignment with the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0; three products are certified for CLR 2.0 and the reference implementation is complete. Industry analysis places mass institutional adoption in 2025–2026, with major platforms — Accredible, Badgr/Canvas Credentials, Credly, POK and others — certifying as 3.0 issuers (Source: POK, Open Badges 3.0 guide).

Standards & Ecosystem Status

• W3C VC 2.0 alignment: Both Open Badges 3.0 and CLR 2.0 are compatible with the Verifiable Credentials model
• Bundling: Individual badges can be packaged into Comprehensive Learner Records and Verifiable Presentations
• University issuance: Approximately 847 universities worldwide were issuing digital credentials as of May 2026 (Source: EveryCRED)

Market Data

• Enterprise adoption: Gartner projects that by 2026, more than 60% of enterprises globally will use verifiable credentials as a core function of their digital identity strategy
• Alternative credentials in higher ed: Expected to grow by $2.62 billion from 2026 to 2030 at an 18.2% CAGR (Source: Technavio)
• Credential management software: From $2.58B in 2025 to $2.91B in 2026, a 12.7% CAGR (Source: The Business Research Company)

Competitive Landscape and Standards Ecosystem

Four Pillars of the Education Standards Ecosystem

Standards for education data, tools, and credentials are evolving through cooperation and division of labor among multiple bodies rather than a single organization. 1EdTech covers the broadest scope — from classroom tool integration to credentials — and complements W3C (identity and verification), Ed-Fi (K-12 data), and IEEE LTSC (metadata).

Key Terminology

1EdTech - Formerly IMS Global. An international nonprofit consortium developing interoperability standards for edtech, stewarding LTI, OneRoster, Open Badges, and more
LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability) - A specification connecting external learning tools to an LMS in a standard way, solving sign-on and grade sync with a single integration
OneRoster - A standard for exchanging class rosters, enrollments, and grades between systems; central to France's decree and the Japan Profile
Open Badges 3.0 - The latest version of the standard for issuing learning achievements as verifiable digital badges, built on the W3C Verifiable Credentials model
CLR (Comprehensive Learner Record) - A standard bundling a learner's full achievements — degrees, badges, competencies — into one verifiable record
Caliper Analytics - A learning analytics specification for collecting and transmitting learning activity data in a standard format; version 1.2 is in beta review
Edu-API - A new API specification standardizing data exchange between higher education administrative systems and teaching and learning systems
TrustEd Apps - 1EdTech's program vetting and certifying edtech products for data privacy and interoperability; includes the Generative AI Data Rubric
