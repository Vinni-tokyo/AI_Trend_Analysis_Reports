📊 Top 5 Trends

Published on December 15, 2025, the 1EdTech "AI-Generated Content Best Practices v1.0" is a community-developed guide involving 50+ contributors from organizations including ETS, Carnegie Learning, D2L, and Digital Promise. Below are the five key trends established by this landmark document.

1. Five-Tier Transparency Labeling Framework: Standardizing GenAI Involvement

Status: 1EdTech has introduced a standardized labeling system that classifies content into five tiers based on the degree of human control over GenAI. This is the industry's first attempt to quantify GenAI's substantive contribution beyond a binary "AI used / not used" disclosure.

Five-Tier Label System

• Level 1: Human-Created, GenAI-Assisted (spell-check, formatting)
• Level 2: Human-Led, GenAI-Enhanced (humans control pedagogy)
• Level 3: GenAI-Co-Created, Human-Guided (joint creation)
• Level 4: GenAI-Dominant, Human-Supervised (AI-led, minimal revision)
• Level 5: Fully AI-Generated (autonomous, not recommended for high-stakes)

Policy Implications

• Portability: Labels persist in QTI/Common Cartridge metadata
• Learner Trust: Research shows clear GenAI disclosure increases educator trust
• Regulatory Alignment: Directly maps to EU AI Act Article 50 labeling obligations
• High-Stakes Restriction: Level 5 is not recommended for assessments or credentialing

2. Risk-Based Oversight Model: Tiered Governance for High/Medium/Low Stakes

Status: Rather than imposing identical validation burdens on all AI content, 1EdTech adopts a risk-based approach that scales oversight intensity according to content's impact on learner outcomes. This aligns directly with the EU AI Act and NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

Three Risk Tiers

• High Risk: Directly affects grades, placement, credentialing → rigorous SME validation, compliance documentation required
• Medium Risk: Shapes instruction but doesn't determine outcomes (practice sets, formative assessments) → human oversight for evaluation
• Low Risk: Enrichment, drafting, translation (no grade impact) → minimal compliance burden

Empirical Evidence

• Error Rates: 30%+ error rates reported in unsupervised GenAI math feedback
• Cost Efficiency: Uniform validation explodes operational costs
• International Alignment: Consistent with EU AI Act 4-tier classification, NIST AI RMF (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage)
• Practicality: Applicable across publishers, educators, students

3. Standards-Aligned Provenance Tracking: QTI, Common Cartridge, LTI, Caliper Integration

Status: AI-generated metadata is embedded into 1EdTech's four established standards (QTI, Common Cartridge, LTI, Caliper) so that provenance and labels persist across system migrations. This is the first standard that resolves the "AI metadata volatility" problem.

Standard-by-Standard Integration

• QTI (Assessment): Capture GenAI annotations in extensible metadata fields
• Common Cartridge: Attach AI labels at the object level in content packages; visibility persists in LMS
• LTI (Tool Integration): Content provider maintains label visibility; LTI serves as access pathway
• Caliper Analytics: Extend event models to track AI-generated content usage

Required Provenance Elements

• GenAI system, model name, version number
• Creation date and timestamps
• Training data reuse declarations
• Human oversight documentation
• Audit trails of revisions
• SME validation records

4. Content Category Differentiation: Publisher, Educator, Student, Hybrid

Status: 1EdTech classifies AI content into four categories, applying differentiated accountability, disclosure, and validation requirements to each. This reflects field feedback that "a single policy cannot cover all actors."

Four Content Categories

• Publisher-Produced: Textbooks, assessments → full provenance, risk assessment, bias audits, SME validation, standards alignment all required
• Educator-Produced: Custom lessons, rubrics → classroom disclosure, brief provenance, teacher validation
• Student-Produced: Essays, projects → student disclosure of AI contributions, alignment with academic integrity
• Hybrid: Co-authored items → multi-point disclosure, dual provenance, stricter oversight

Subject-Specific Considerations

• Mathematics: Strict equation formatting, computational accuracy verification
• Languages: Linguistic precision, cultural sensitivity, target-language fidelity
• Science: Verification against authoritative sources, terminology consistency
• Social Studies: Fact-checking, multi-perspective presentation, bias mitigation

📖 Key Terminology

1EdTech (formerly IMS Global): Global non-profit consortium that develops EdTech interoperability standards, including QTI, Common Cartridge, LTI, Caliper, and Open Badges.
Provenance: Metadata system tracking the origin, creation history, and validation records of content. For AI content, this includes model name, version, and SME validation status.
SME (Subject Matter Expert): Domain expert responsible for validating the academic accuracy and pedagogical appropriateness of AI-generated content.
QTI (Question & Test Interoperability): 1EdTech's standard for assessment content interoperability. Supports embedding AI metadata in extensible fields.
Common Cartridge: 1EdTech's learning content packaging standard. Allows AI labels to be attached at the object level and made visible in LMS.
LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability): 1EdTech's standard for learning tool integration. Maintains label persistence when external content is integrated into LMS.
Caliper Analytics: 1EdTech's learning analytics standard. Extended to track AI content usage events.
EU AI Act Article 50: Transparency obligations for AI system providers and deployers. Mandatory labeling and watermarking of AI-generated content takes effect August 2026.
WCAG 2.1 AA: Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Used as the standard for validating accuracy of AI-generated alt-text and captions.
