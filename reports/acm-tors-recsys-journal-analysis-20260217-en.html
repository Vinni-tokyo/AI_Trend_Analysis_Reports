Top 5 Key Trends

1. The Birth of ACM TORS: Historical Significance of the First Dedicated RecSys Journal

Current Status: ACM Transactions on Recommender Systems (ACM TORS) is the world's first and only academic journal exclusively dedicated to recommender systems research. Officially announced by ACM in March 2022, its inaugural issue (Volume 1, Issue 1) was published on January 27, 2023. The journal was conceived by Editor-in-Chief Dietmar Jannach (University of Klagenfurt) in summer 2020, inspired by a blog post asking "Why is there no International Journal on Recommender Systems?" Together with co-EiC Li Chen (Hong Kong Baptist University), a proposal was submitted to the ACM RecSys Steering Committee. The journal's launch coincided with the ACM RecSys conference being elevated to CORE 'A' ranking in 2021, signaling the field's maturity.

Journal Specifications

• EISSN: 2770-6699
• Frequency: Quarterly (4 issues per year)
• Publisher: Association for Computing Machinery (ACM)

• Official Launch: March 2022, ACM public announcement
• Inaugural Issue: January 2023, Volume 1, Issue 1

2. Large Language Models Meet Recommender Systems

Current Status: ACM TORS organized a special issue on "Large Language Models for Recommender Systems (LLM4RecSys)" in 2024, spotlighting the frontier of LLM-powered recommendation research. Guest editors Yongfeng Zhang (Rutgers University), Lei Li (Hong Kong Baptist University), and Luyang Kong (Amazon Research) led this initiative, reflecting how the integration of LLMs with recommender systems has become a central research theme across both academia and industry.

Key LLM4RecSys Research Directions

• Conversational Recommendation: Natural language interfaces powered by LLMs
• Explainable Recommendation: Leveraging LLM text generation for recommendation justification
• Multimodal Recommendation: Integrating text, images, and reviews for holistic recommendations
• Prompt Engineering: Designing task-optimized prompts for recommendation

This research direction is expected to accelerate as generative AI continues reshaping the recommendation landscape.

3. Responsible Recommender Systems: Fairness, Trustworthiness, and Ethics

Current Status: ACM TORS is leading the academic discourse on the societal impact of recommender systems. Following a 2023 special issue on "Trustworthy Recommender Systems," the journal launched a 2024 special issue on "Recommender Systems for Good." Guest editors Marko Tkalčič, Noemi Mauro, Alan Said, Nava Tintarev, and Antonela Tommasel curated research exploring how recommender systems can promote healthier behaviors, support education for low-income families, encourage energy-saving habits, enable fair micro-lending, and improve news consumption quality.

Special Issue Progression

• 2023: Causal Inference for RecSys
• 2023: Trustworthy Recommender Systems
• 2024: Recommender Systems for Good
• 2025: Papers on sensitive attribute bias and design justice approaches

Core Research Topics

• Fairness: Equitable recommendations across diverse stakeholders
• Transparency: Explainability of recommendation rationale
• Bias Mitigation: Removing sensitive attribute association bias
• Design Justice: Recommendation design considering marginalized communities

Outlook: With the EU AI Act and other global AI regulations tightening, academic research on the ethical dimensions of recommender systems will see increasing demand. ACM TORS is well-positioned as the key publication venue, providing the scholarly foundation that industry needs for regulatory compliance strategies.

4. Recommender Systems in Industry: Challenges and Real-World Solutions

Current Status: In 2024, ACM TORS organized a special issue on "Recommender Systems in Industry: Challenges and Solutions," explicitly working to bridge the gap between academic research and industrial practice. This initiative addresses the latest advances and ongoing challenges in designing and implementing recommender systems in real-world environments. The industrial significance is substantial: Amazon attributes 35% of its revenue to recommendation engines, and 31% of e-commerce revenue overall comes from personalized recommendations.

Key Industry Application Areas

• E-commerce: 31% of revenue from personalized recommendations (Amazon: 35%)
• Streaming/Entertainment: Content discovery for Netflix, Spotify, etc.
• Healthcare: 19% CAGR growth projected through 2030
• Education: Adaptive learning path recommendation

Bridging the Industry-Academia Gap

• Scalability: Lab experiments vs. real-time serving for hundreds of millions
• A/B Testing: Discrepancy between offline evaluation and online performance
• Cold Start: Strategies for new users and items
• Real-time Feedback: Instant adaptation from streaming data

Particularly noteworthy are scholarly solutions for practical challenges such as causal inference-based promotion effectiveness measurement and long-tail item personalization.

5. New Directions for News Recommender Systems

Current Status: In December 2025, ACM TORS issued a call for papers for a special issue on "New Directions for News Recommender Systems." Guest editors Joseph Konstan (University of Minnesota), Özlem Özgöbek (Norwegian University of Science and Technology), and Edward Malthouse (Northwestern University) are leading this initiative. The special issue seeks novel recommendation paradigms that move beyond click optimization for rapidly expiring content.

Core Challenges in News Recommendation

• Content Lifespan: News loses value within hours
• Beyond Clickbait: Quality metrics beyond click-through rate (CTR)
• Filter Bubbles: Information bias and echo chamber effects
• Multilingual Support: Cross-language news recommendation algorithms

Related TORS Research Trends

• Design Justice Approach: Democratic design methodology for news recommendation
• Music Repetition Modeling: Extended content consumption pattern research
• Slate Recommendation: Reward-informed bandit approaches
• Video Game Translation: Domain-extending recommendation research

ACM TORS is playing a pioneering role in establishing the scholarly foundation for this critical domain.

Key Terminology

ACM TORS (ACM Transactions on Recommender Systems) - The world's first journal exclusively dedicated to recommender systems research, published by ACM. Covers algorithms, user experience, evaluation, and societal impact comprehensively.
Collaborative Filtering - A recommendation technique that suggests items based on behavioral data from users with similar preferences. Played a pivotal role in the Netflix Prize competition.
LLM4RecSys - The research area integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with recommender systems. An ACM TORS special issue topic, applied in conversational and explainable recommendation.
CORE Ranking - A conference ranking system operated by the Computing Research and Education Association of Australasia. Conferences are ranked as A*, A, B, or C. ACM RecSys achieved 'A' ranking in 2021.
H-index - A metric measuring the impact of a journal or researcher. An H-index of h means h papers have each been cited at least h times. ACM TORS currently has an H-index of 9.
Impact Factor (IF) - A journal influence metric calculated by Clarivate's Journal Citation Reports, based on the average citations of papers published in the previous two years. ACM TORS has not yet been assigned an IF as a new journal.
Slate Recommendation - A technique that optimizes an entire list (slate) of items rather than individual items. Applied in news feeds, music playlists, and similar contexts.
