The Competency Graph: Global Research and Market Trends

Knowledge graphs linking occupations, skills, and credentials — the state of research, industry, and standards
Published: May 22, 2026 | Standard Report | ID: TR-20260522-001

Top 5 Trends

1. Standardization of Open Competency Ontologies — ESCO, O*NET, WEF

Status: Open ontologies that formally define occupations, skills, and qualifications have become the de facto global coordinate system. The EU's ESCO publishes occupations, skills, and qualifications in 27 languages as SKOS/RDF; the U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET standardizes attributes for roughly 1,016 occupations. Together with the WEF Global Skills Taxonomy, these serve as the foundational nodes of competency graphs in both enterprise HR and academia.

Key Features

• ESCO: 27 languages, open SKOS/RDF, occupation-skill-qualification links
• O*NET: U.S. government standard, job-attribute database
• WEF: global skills taxonomy, future-of-work signals

Why It Matters

• Provides a shared vocabulary across disparate data sources
• Enables semantic search rather than keyword matching
• Combines ontology (schema) + data (instances) into a knowledge graph

📊 Credibility: 9/10
Strengths: ESCO/O*NET are verifiable government/EU primary sources · Weaknesses: WEF taxonomy adoption rates need further evidence
🔗 Related Links: ESCO | O*NET OnLine | World Economic Forum

Outlook: Open ontologies will increasingly interlink as Linked Open Data, becoming the standard starting point for any organization building its own competency graph.

2. The Commercial Competency-Graph Race — Talent Intelligence

Status: Major HR-tech vendors now compete on proprietary graphs as their core asset. Eightfold claims 1.6 million skills derived from 1.6 billion career trajectories; Gloat runs the "Workforce Graph (Loomra)," Beamery the "Talent Graph," and LinkedIn the "Skills Graph." The graph itself has become the competitive moat.

Key Players

• Eightfold: 1.6M skills, talent intelligence
• Gloat: Workforce Graph, internal talent marketplace
• Beamery / LinkedIn: Talent Graph / Skills Graph

Market Size

• Talent intelligence software: $8.98B (2023) → $27.82B (2033), CAGR 11.97%
• Skills intelligence platforms: $3.5B (2023) → $9.2B (2029)
• Gartner: 60% of large enterprises to adopt AI skills marketplaces by 2025

📊 Credibility: 8/10
Strengths: Size/CAGR figures cited from multiple market-research firms · Weaknesses: Wide ranges due to differing market definitions
🔗 Related Links: Eightfold | Gloat | Beamery | LinkedIn Talent

Outlook: Alongside the data-scale race, the quality of GNN- and LLM-based recommendations running on top of the graph will become the next axis of differentiation.

3. The Rise of GNN-Based Personalized Recommendation Research

Status: Research applying Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on occupation-skill graphs for personalized job and learning recommendations is thriving. In 2024, HRGraph used an LLM to extract entities, build an HR knowledge graph, and perform information-propagation-based job recommendation; ReCareer combined a hybrid GNN with dynamic gating re-ranking. RecSysHR 2024 featured a dynamic Jobs-Skills KG built on ISCO/ESCO/O*NET.

Representative Work

• HRGraph (arXiv 2024): LLM entity extraction → HR KG → job rec
• ReCareer (Springer): hybrid GNN + dynamic gating re-rank
• Dynamic Jobs-Skills KG (RecSysHR 2024)

Core Techniques

• Link prediction: quantify skill-occupation relevance
• Path finding: design career paths via shortest paths
• Heterogeneous graphs: learn over occupations, skills, and badges together

📊 Credibility: 9/10
Strengths: Direct citations of peer-reviewed/open papers (arXiv, Springer, CEUR) · Weaknesses: Industry performance metrics vary by paper
🔗 Related Links: HRGraph (arXiv) | Dynamic Jobs-Skills KG | Job Posting-Enriched KG

Outlook: Learned recommendation atop static ontologies will become standard, with growing emphasis on explainability — justifying "why" via graph paths.

4. The Shift to Skills-First Hiring and Internal Mobility

Status: The center of gravity in hiring and internal mobility is shifting from degrees to skills. The competency graph is the engine of this shift, matching jobs and people at the skill level. Organizations deploying internal talent marketplaces report ROI such as up to 20 fewer days to fill a role, 3–5x lower hiring cost, and a 6x larger available talent pool.

What's Changing

• Removal/relaxation of degree requirements
• Roles redefined as bundles of skills
• Internal moves, gigs, and mentoring matched via the graph

Reported Impact

• Time-to-fill cut by up to 20 days
• Hiring cost reduced 3–5x
• Talent pool expanded 6x, improved retention

📊 Credibility: 7/10
Strengths: Adoption impact stated as quantitative metrics · Weaknesses: ROI figures are vendor-reported and warrant independent validation
🔗 Related Links: Talent Marketplace (Gloat) | Mercer Skills Snapshot

Outlook: Both public and private sectors will accelerate skills-first hiring, raising demand to connect verified credentials (badges) to the graph.

5. Ontology + LLM Convergence (GraphRAG, Explainable Coaching)

Status: To curb LLM hallucination, the GraphRAG pattern is spreading: a deterministic knowledge graph anchors the facts while the LLM handles narrative and coaching. As in research on competency ontologies for learning personalization (Smart Learning Environments), a clear division is settling in — the graph defines "what is needed," and the LLM explains "how to get there."

Core Idea

• KG = deterministic facts (gaps, required skills)
• LLM = natural-language coaching and narrative
• Recommendation rationale explained via graph paths

Benefits

• Hallucination control, verifiable recommendations
• Reflects personal context (held skills, goals)
• Personalized learning paths can lift completion and mastery

📊 Credibility: 8/10
Strengths: Backed by both academic research and industry patterns · Weaknesses: Quantitative "completion lift" claims need context-specific validation
🔗 Related Links: Competency Ontology (SLE) | ESCO Ontology Model

Outlook: Graph-LLM hybrids will become the standard architecture for career and learning coaching services.

  • Open standards (ESCO/O*NET) are converging as the shared base for all commercial graphs
  • Competition is moving from data scale (skill counts) to recommendation quality (GNN, explainability)
  • A B2C gap is emerging in individual learning and credentials beyond B2B internal mobility

Application Ideas

Observed approaches in the market.

2. GraphRAG gap analysis - Diagnose skill gaps deterministically via the graph while the LLM generates personalized coaching narrative

3. Credential layer linkage - Map Open Badges and micro-credentials to skill nodes to offer "verified learning paths"

Executive Summary

Key Findings

  • Validated concept: The competency graph is a core trend in both research and industry — major HR-tech vendors treat their graph as a flagship asset
  • Growing market: Talent intelligence software projected at $27.8B by 2033 (CAGR ~12%); Gartner expects 60% of large enterprises to adopt AI skills marketplaces by 2025
  • Tech convergence: Open ontology (foundation) + GNN (personalization) + LLM (explanation) — a three-layer pattern
  • Emerging gap: Incumbents focus on B2B internal mobility — individual (B2C) learning and credentials remain relatively open space

Positioning Guide

Head-on competition on data scale → unfavorable
Reason: incumbents lead on data scale (e.g., 1.6M skills vs. ~15k)
Narrow, distinct position (Open Badge-based individual learning paths) → promising
Reason: B2C, credential verification, and learning paths are under-served by incumbents; the graph is the basis for differentiation

Overall sentiment: Positive (concept validated + market growth + clear room to differentiate)

Key Terminology

Ontology - A formal definition of what concepts exist and how they may relate (schema / T-Box).
Knowledge Graph - An ontology (schema) populated with actual entities (instances / A-Box).
Taxonomy - A lightweight hierarchical classification (broader/narrower only); an ontology adds arbitrary relations.
GNN (Graph Neural Network) - A network that learns node representations by aggregating neighbor information; graph structure itself is an input feature.
GraphRAG - A retrieval-augmented pattern where a knowledge graph anchors facts and an LLM generates the answer on top.
Micro-credential / Open Badge - A verifiable digital credential proving acquisition of a specific skill.
