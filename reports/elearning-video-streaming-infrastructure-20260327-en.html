Key Terminology

Adaptive Bitrate Streaming (ABR) — A technique that automatically adjusts video quality based on network conditions. It switches to high quality on Wi-Fi and lower quality on cellular data, ensuring uninterrupted playback regardless of connection speed.
CDN (Content Delivery Network) — A globally distributed network of servers that delivers content from the location closest to the user. CDNs dramatically improve loading speed and reliability for educational video.
WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication) — An open standard that enables real-time audio and video communication directly in the browser without plugins. It powers services like Zoom and Google Meet and is widely used for live lectures in education.
DRM (Digital Rights Management) — Technology that prevents unauthorized copying and distribution of digital content. Widevine (Google), FairPlay (Apple), and PlayReady (Microsoft) are the three major standards, and DRM is essential for protecting premium online courses.
HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) — A video streaming protocol developed by Apple that divides video into small segments delivered over HTTP. It is supported by virtually all devices and browsers, making it the de facto industry standard.
Transcoding — The process of converting a single source video into multiple resolutions, bitrates, and formats. Transcoding creates the various versions needed to play educational video smoothly across different devices.
MOQ (Media over QUIC) — A next-generation media transport protocol built on Google's QUIC protocol. It offers lower latency and greater network stability than WebRTC, making it well-suited for large-scale live streaming.
