1. Formal "Textbook" Status for Digital Textbooks by 2030

Current Status: The Central Council for Education's Digital Textbook Promotion Working Group, under MEXT, published its deliberation summary on September 24, 2025, confirming the policy to institutionalize digital textbooks - currently classified as "supplementary materials" - as formal "textbooks" on equal footing with paper textbooks. Accordingly, the textbook authorization, adoption, and free distribution systems will all be extended to cover digital textbooks. An amendment to the School Education Act is scheduled for submission to the regular session of the Diet in 2026, with formal introduction timed to coincide with the next Course of Study revision in 2030 (Source: Nikkei xTECH, 2025).

Key Institutional Changes

• Current: Digital textbooks classified as "supplementary materials"
• After Amendment: Elevated to formal "textbook" status
• Authorization Scope: QR code-linked content also subject to authorization review
• Free Distribution: Provided free of charge by the national government, same as paper

Three Textbook Types

• Paper Textbook: Maintains the existing format
• Digital Textbook: Tablet/PC-exclusive format
• Hybrid Textbook: Paper core content + digital supplementary materials
• Selection Authority: Municipal boards of education choose from the three types

Current Status: On June 13, 2025, the Digital Agency, MIC, MEXT, and METI jointly published the "Education DX Roadmap." 4. Health Impact and Safety Measures

Current Status: Health impacts on students from digital textbook adoption are a central concern in Japan's education sector. Key issues include declining eyesight, eye strain, and posture problems. MEXT recommends countermeasures such as health monitoring in coordination with school physicians, posture guidance, and ensuring adequate rest periods. Scientific evidence gathering on prolonged screen exposure is also underway.

Key Health Concerns

• Declining Eyesight: Increased myopia from prolonged screen viewing
• Eye Strain: Blue light exposure and screen flicker
• Posture Problems: Cervical/spinal strain from tablet use
• Concentration: Digital distraction effects

Countermeasures and Guidelines

• School Physicians: Regular health status checks in coordination
• Posture Guidance: Proper posture and screen distance education
• Rest Rules: Eye breaks from screens every 30 minutes
• Paper Integration: Hybrid approach to distribute screen time

5. Teacher ICT Competency Gaps and Training Challenges

Current Status: The gap in teachers' information literacy (ICT literacy) has been identified as a major challenge for digital textbook utilization. Differences in ICT competency among teachers directly translate into disparities in "teaching quality," resulting in wide variation in utilization levels across schools even when using the same digital textbooks. Strengthening teacher training programs has been prioritized as the top task in GIGA School Phase 2.

Teacher-Related Challenges

• ICT Gaps: Wide variation in information literacy among teachers
• Teaching Quality: Disparities in digital tool-integrated instruction skills
• System Failures: Ability to handle in-class freezes and technical issues
• Generational Divide: Digital transition burden on veteran teachers

Response Strategies

• Training Programs: Enhanced ICT-integrated lesson design training
• ICT Support Staff: Deployment of technical support personnel at each school
• Best Practices: Platform for sharing pioneering school case studies
• Phased Introduction: Gradual expansion matched to teacher readiness
