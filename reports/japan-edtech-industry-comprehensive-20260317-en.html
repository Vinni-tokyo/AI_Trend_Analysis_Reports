Top 5 Key Trends

1. K-12 — GIGA School Phase 2 and the Evolution of AI Learning Platforms

Current State: The second phase of Japan's landmark GIGA School Initiative has fundamentally transformed the K-12 technology landscape. According to the latest data from MM Research Institute, ChromeOS has surged to a commanding 60% market share in Phase 2 device procurement, an 18-point increase from its Phase 1 position. This remarkable gain came largely at the expense of Microsoft Windows, which suffered a dramatic 19-point decline to just 10% share, while Apple's iPadOS maintained a steady 31% foothold. With approximately 4.75 million terminal devices scheduled for replacement and 72% of those already refreshed during FY2025, the Phase 2 transition is proceeding at pace. The average device unit price has settled at approximately JPY 55,000 (roughly $370), reflecting the market's prioritization of cost optimization. Looking ahead, the "NEXT GIGA" initiative will commence a comprehensive 4-year public cloud migration plan beginning in 2026, marking a decisive shift from device-centric to cloud-centric educational ICT infrastructure.

Simultaneously, Japan's AI-powered learning platform market has matured around three dominant players collectively known as the "AI Big Three." atama+ has expanded its AI-driven personalized learning system to over 4,000 classrooms nationwide and launched a franchise model in June 2024, converting all 29 of its own locations to the atama+ brand by spring 2025. Study Sapuri, developed by Recruit Holdings, has achieved penetration in 2,322 of Japan's approximately 5,000 high schools and introduced AI-powered subtitles for its video lectures in February 2025, advancing universal design accessibility. Qubena, specializing in AI-powered tablet learning materials for elementary and middle schools, is planning to launch content for four additional elective subjects in FY2026, significantly broadening the scope of adaptive learning.

2. Higher Education — Generative AI Integration and University DX Acceleration

Current State: Japan's higher education sector is navigating an unprecedented period of digital transformation, driven by the convergence of generative AI adoption, government policy mandates, and competitive pressure from global institutions. In December 2024, the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) published its "Generative AI Guidelines Ver.2.0," establishing a comprehensive framework for how universities should govern AI usage in teaching, research, and assessment. This second iteration introduced nuanced guidance on plagiarism detection, academic integrity protocols, and recommended pedagogical approaches for incorporating AI tools such as ChatGPT and Claude into curricula. In response, major national universities including the University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Tohoku University have developed institution-specific AI policies, while private universities like Waseda and Keio have established dedicated AI literacy courses as part of their general education requirements.

The infrastructure backbone of university DX is also evolving rapidly. Virtual reality classrooms have emerged as a notable innovation, with AOMINEXT's VR virtual school platform enabling immersive remote lectures and laboratory simulations. Traditional Learning Management Systems (LMS) remain central to operations, with Moodle and Classi commanding the largest install base, but institutions are increasingly demanding interoperability between systems — connecting LMS data with student information systems, library databases, and career services portals. However, a persistent IT talent shortage within university administrative structures continues to constrain the pace of digital transformation. According to HoloNIQ's 2026 Global EdTech survey, Japanese universities rank among the top adopters of AI governance frameworks in Asia but lag behind in implementation velocity due to organizational and staffing bottlenecks.

• National Universities: University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Tohoku University each published institutional AI policies by Q1 2025
• Private Universities: Waseda and Keio introduced mandatory AI literacy modules in general education curricula starting April 2025
• Assessment Reform: Shift from traditional examinations toward portfolio-based and project-based assessment to mitigate AI-enabled plagiarism risks
• Faculty Development: Over 200 universities conducted generative AI training workshops for faculty during FY2025

3. Corporate Training — Explosive Growth in Reskilling and AI Training Demand

Current State: Japan's corporate training and e-learning market has entered a period of exceptional growth, propelled by the dual pressures of a rapidly aging workforce and the government's ambitious reskilling agenda. The domestic e-learning market reached JPY 381.2 billion in FY2024 and is projected to grow to JPY 384.9 billion in FY2025, with particularly strong demand in AI/digital skills training. The Japanese government has committed an unprecedented JPY 1 trillion over five years to reskilling programs, the largest such investment in the nation's history, reflecting the urgency of preparing the workforce for an AI-augmented economy. This domestic momentum mirrors global trends: the worldwide corporate e-learning market is forecast to expand from $125.6 billion to $335 billion, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 21.7%. Even more dramatically, the global AI in EdTech market is projected to surge from $3.65 billion to $92.09 billion at a CAGR of 38.1%, underscoring the transformative role AI plays across all education and training segments.

At the platform level, Japanese corporate training providers are leveraging AI to fundamentally reshape engagement and completion metrics. Schoo, one of Japan's leading online business learning platforms, implemented generative AI-powered course recommendation and personalization features that boosted course completion rates from under 40% to 65% — a transformative improvement in an industry where low completion rates have long been the norm. Cloud-based LMS platforms have become the default infrastructure for enterprise training, displacing legacy on-premise solutions. Major providers including Schoo, Udemy Business Japan, and Globis Unlimited have reported surging demand for AI literacy courses, data science fundamentals, and prompt engineering workshops. The trend toward "learning in the flow of work" — embedding micro-learning modules directly into productivity tools like Slack and Microsoft Teams — is gaining traction among technology-forward enterprises.

4. Lifelong Learning — Recurrent Education and the Democratization of Online Learning

Current State: Japan's lifelong learning landscape presents a paradox: while the country's overall education market is contracting due to demographic decline (Japan's population fell below 124 million in 2025 for the first time in decades), the EdTech-enabled lifelong learning segment is experiencing explosive growth. The global online education market is forecast to expand from $3.6 billion in 2025 to $29.3 billion by 2034, representing a CAGR of 26.13%. Within Japan, the recurrent education market — programs designed for working adults returning to formal or semi-formal learning — reached JPY 49 billion in FY2022 and has been growing steadily since. According to Nomura Research Institute (NRI), Japan's overall EdTech market (including lifelong learning) is projected to exceed JPY 320 billion by 2025, reflecting strong demand across adult learning categories despite the overall demographic headwinds.

The platform ecosystem serving lifelong learners has become increasingly sophisticated. Schoo has established itself as Japan's dominant domestic platform for business and professional development, offering over 8,000 courses with AI-powered personalization. Udemy Japan (operated through a partnership with Benesse) provides access to the global Udemy catalog with localized content and Japanese-language instruction, particularly strong in technology and creative skills. Coursera has expanded its Japanese-language offerings and partnerships with Japanese universities, offering degree programs and professional certificates that are gaining recognition from major employers. The government's push for "recurrent education" — lifelong learning programs that enable career transitions and upskilling — is reinforced by tax incentives for individuals and subsidies for employers who support employee learning. The rise of micro-credentials, digital badges, and stackable certificates is making non-traditional learning pathways increasingly viable in Japan's traditionally credentialist labor market.

5. Technology Pillars — AI, Cloud, and XR Convergence in Education Infrastructure

Current State: The technological foundation underpinning Japan's entire EdTech ecosystem is undergoing a massive scale-up, driven by unprecedented levels of investment in cloud infrastructure, AI compute capacity, and immersive technologies. Oracle Japan has committed over $8 billion to strengthening its cloud and AI infrastructure in Japan, positioning itself as the enterprise cloud backbone for major education companies like Benesse. Microsoft has announced approximately $2.9 billion in AI infrastructure investment in Japan, with significant portions earmarked for Azure OpenAI Service deployments in education and research contexts. KDDI is advancing its Osaka Sakai data center project utilizing NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 GPU clusters for liquid-cooled, high-density AI computing, with services launching in early 2026. SoftBank is building out over 150MW of AI compute capacity, making it one of Japan's largest private AI infrastructure investors.

At the policy level, the AI Promotion Act, enacted on May 28, 2025, provides the institutional and legal framework for AI adoption across all sectors including education. The FY2025 national budget allocated approximately JPY 196.9 billion (roughly $1.3 billion) for AI-related infrastructure and initiatives. In the academic sphere, GakuNin Cloud continues to serve as the federated cloud access platform for universities and research institutions, with participating institutions including Hokkaido University and others increasingly adopting zero-trust security architectures as they migrate from legacy network-segregated environments. AWS has launched education-specific AI solutions, including the Education AI Assistant and TP2 (Teaching Plan Personalizer), demonstrated at AWS Summit Japan 2025. The next-generation ABCI 3.0 (AI Bridging Cloud Infrastructure) supercomputer, operated by AIST, represents Japan's bid to maintain AI research competitiveness at the global level. In immersive technology, VR/XR applications are expanding from experimental pilots into production use cases, with medical education, engineering simulation, and language learning emerging as the highest-ROI deployment scenarios. Japan currently accounts for 5.3% of the global EdTech market, which is estimated at $187.1 billion in 2025.

Key Terminology

GIGA School Initiative — Japan's MEXT-led national program providing one computing device per student alongside high-speed network infrastructure. Phase 2 (2025-2026) focuses on device refresh and cloud migration, with ChromeOS capturing 60% market share.
NEXT GIGA — The next stage of the GIGA School Initiative, beginning in 2026, which prioritizes a complete 4-year migration to public cloud environments, shifting the education ICT paradigm from device-centric to cloud-centric.
Adaptive Learning — A learning approach where AI analyzes learner comprehension and progress to automatically deliver individually optimized materials and exercises. atama+, Study Sapuri, and Qubena are representative implementations in Japan's K-12 market.
Zero-Trust Security — A security architecture that eliminates the assumption that internal networks are inherently safe, verifying every access request regardless of origin. Increasingly adopted in education as institutions migrate from network-segregated environments to cloud infrastructure.
GakuNin Cloud — A federated cloud service platform provided by Japan's Academic Authentication Federation (GakuNin) for universities and research institutions, enabling secure multi-cloud access and identity management.
AI Promotion Act — Japanese legislation enacted on May 28, 2025, establishing the legal and institutional framework for responsible AI adoption across sectors including education, healthcare, and public services. Backed by JPY 196.9B in FY2025 budget allocation.
Recurrent Education — A Japanese policy framework promoting lifelong learning for working adults, enabling career transitions and skill upgrades through subsidized programs. MEXT's "Brush Up Program" covers up to 70% of eligible learning costs.
Reskilling — The process of training workers in new skills to prepare for changing job requirements, particularly in the context of AI and digital transformation. Japan's government has committed JPY 1 trillion over 5 years to national reskilling programs.
LMS (Learning Management System) — Software platforms that manage, deliver, and track educational content and learner progress. Moodle (open-source) and Classi (Japan-developed) are the dominant LMS platforms in Japanese higher education.
Micro-Credentials / Digital Badges — Granular, verifiable digital certifications that represent mastery of specific skills or competencies. Increasingly recognized by Japanese employers as alternatives to traditional credentials for hiring and promotion decisions.
