Top 5 Analysis Points

1. What the TrustEd Credential Coalition Is: A Trust Framework for the Badge Quality Gap

Current status: 1EdTech's TrustEd Credential Coalition was formed to address "varying levels of quality and trust in digital credentials." First unveiled as the TrustEd Microcredential Framework at the Digital Credentials Summit on March 5, 2024, the initiative has since grown into the TrustEd Credential Coalition covering credentials broadly, with the expanded framework finalized on May 12, 2026. Today more than 43 member organizations — spanning K-12 districts, universities, technology companies, and workforce organizations — participate, and 34 organizations including Google, Oracle, Instructure, Cengage, and WGU have signed the endorsement commitment.

Why It Was Formed

• The quality gap: As badge issuance boomed, quality and rigor varied wildly, eroding trust among receivers (employers, universities)
• Goal: Make credential rigor explicit through metadata and give learners agency over their credentials
• Evolution: TrustEd Microcredential Coalition (2024) → expanded scope as the TrustEd Credential Coalition

Participation

• Members: 43+ organizations across K-12, higher education, tech providers, and workforce
• 34 signatories: Google, Oracle, Instructure, Cengage, Anthology, WGU, Udemy, the California State University System, NYC Department of Education, Digital Promise, and more
• How to join: Open to 1EdTech Contributing Members

2. The Credential Taxonomy and the "One-Way Rule"

Current status: The framework classifies credentials by how learners demonstrate competency: TrustEd Application credentials earned by applying knowledge through practical work (lab projects, research presentations), TrustEd Knowledge credentials earned by demonstrating foundational knowledge through validated assessments (course exams), and Recognition Badges for event attendance or membership with no formal assessment. The core rule is simple and strict: miss even one required field and a credential cannot carry the "TrustEd Credential" name. Recommended fields can be omitted without losing the name — they raise learner value when present. Issuers may add metadata beyond the requirements freely, but dropping below any required field forfeits the designation: a one-way rule.

Three Credential Types

• TrustEd Application: Earned by applying knowledge — lab work, projects, research presentations — with the completed artifact attached as evidence
• TrustEd Knowledge: Earned by demonstrating foundational knowledge through validated assessment, with exam results attached as evidence
• Recognition Badge: Webinar attendance, membership, etc. — outside the scope of TrustEd requirements

What the One-Way Rule Means

• The naming gate: All 8 required fields satisfied = eligible to use "TrustEd Credential"
• Recommended fields: The name is kept without them; meeting them increases learner value
• Freedom above the bar: Extra metadata beyond the requirements does not affect TrustEd status
• Disqualification: Falling below even one required field immediately forfeits the name

Weaknesses: Operational details of the one-way rule (handling of violations, etc.) should be re-verified once the certification process is finalized

Outlook: The Application-versus-Knowledge distinction gives hiring teams an instant signal — "hands-on" versus "knowledge-based" credentials — which should lower screening costs in skills-first hiring.

3. The 11 Metadata Fields: 8 Required + 3 Recommended

Current status: The metadata requirements for issuing a TrustEd Credential comprise 11 fields — 8 Required and 3 Recommended. Each field maps to either a native Open Badges 3.0 capability or an OBEE (Open Badges Extensions for Education) extension.

Framework AlignmentRequiredLink to external frameworks such as CASE, CTDL, or Rich Skills DescriptorsOB 3.0 core Alignment class
Issuer AccreditationRequiredState the issuer's accreditation and authority to issueOBEE Issuer Accreditation Extension
EvidenceRequiredAttach at least one evidence artifactOB 3.0 Evidence

ResultRequiredAt least one result with a date (need not be numeric)OB 3.0 Result
CriteriaRequiredState the criteria required for achievementOB 3.0 Criteria
AssessmentRequiredDescription of the assessment and its dateOBEE Assessment Extension (attached to ResultDescription)
DurationRecommendedCredit or time-to-complete informationExtended metadata
Achievement typeRequiredSpecify the closest specific type (N/A not allowed)OB 3.0 achievementType
EndorsementRecommendedThird-party endorsement or approvalOB 3.0 Endorsement

What the 8 Required Fields Guarantee

• What (Skills, Alignment): Which skills were demonstrated, against which public framework
• Who (Issuer Accreditation): What authority or accreditation stands behind the issuer
• How (Evidence, Result, Criteria, Assessment): What criteria were assessed and what evidence and results remain
• As what (Achievement type): Exactly what kind of credential this is — N/A is not allowed

The Value of the 3 Recommended Fields

• Rubric: Publishing scoring criteria strengthens assessment transparency — implemented not as an OBEE extension but via OB 3.0's native ResultDescription
• Duration: Credit and time information makes learning effort comparable across credentials
• Endorsement: Third-party approval adds market trust to the credential

4. The Technical Foundation: Open Badges 3.0 + OBEE Extensions + CLR 2.0

Current status: The requirements rest on three technical layers. First, Open Badges 3.0 — built on W3C Verifiable Credentials — guarantees the credential's own authenticity and natively supports many required fields via the Alignment class, Result, Criteria, and achievementType. Second, OBEE (Open Badges Extensions for Education) supplies the Issuer Accreditation Extension for accreditation information and the Assessment Extension for assessment details. OBEE began as a university-system-led initiative, with v1.0 published as Candidate Final in 2018; according to the provided source material, OB 3.0-compatible versions (v2.0) are being prepared. Third, integration with CLR 2.0 lets TrustEd credentials be bundled into a comprehensive learner record.

The Role of the OBEE Extensions

• Issuer Accreditation Extension: References one or more accreditation bodies certifying the badge issuer — expressing "who vouches for the issuer's authority" in machine-readable form
• Assessment Extension: Describes the assessment(s) — description, method, date — completed as part of earning the badge
• Origin: A university-led initiative to give badges transcript-level trust (v1.0 Candidate Final, 2018)

The Layered Stack

• W3C VC/DID: The foundation of cryptographic verifiability
• Open Badges 3.0: The credential data model (Alignment, Result, Criteria, Evidence native)
• OBEE extensions: Education-specific metadata (issuer accreditation, assessment)
• TrustEd framework: A policy layer of "minimum requirements" over the combination
• CLR 2.0: Bundling credentials into comprehensive records

Strengths: The OBEE v1.0 extension specs and the OB 3.0 spec are confirmed from official documents

5. What Issuers Must Do: The Commitment and the Gap Analysis

Current status: Endorsing organizations make three commitments: (1) adhere to the framework's minimum metadata expectations to ensure consistency across all classification levels; (2) actively implement — or require partners to implement — validation and certification programs, notably Open Badges 3.0 compliance; and (3) actively engage stakeholders to raise awareness of digital credential quality and value. Beyond the commitment, the Coalition is developing a certification process to verify credential trustworthiness. In practice, an issuer's first step is a gap analysis: mapping each of its credentials against the 11 fields as "compliant / needs metadata extension / partially compliant / needs confirmation" reveals where to invest first.

The Three Commitment Clauses

• Metadata adherence: Meet the framework's minimum expectations consistently across all classification levels
• Certification: Implement validation and certification programs (including OB 3.0 compliance) or require partners to do so
• Awareness: Communicate credential quality and value and engage stakeholders

Typical Gap-Analysis Patterns

• Often already compliant: Skills, Result, Criteria — commonly captured in standard OB issuance flows
• Often needing extension: Framework Alignment, Issuer Accreditation, Assessment — require the Alignment class and OBEE extensions
• Needing operational design: Evidence — issuers must build a process to collect evidence artifacts at issuance time

Weaknesses: Details of the certification process (review method, display) are still in development and should be revisited on publication

Major Quality-Assurance Approaches

Multiple frameworks answer the question "which credentials can be trusted?" through different approaches. TrustEd is distinctive as a practice-oriented approach anchored directly in the technical standard: minimum metadata requirements at issuance time.

Executive Summary

Key Findings

  • A quality gate: A credential may carry the TrustEd name only if all 8 required metadata fields are satisfied — missing even one forfeits it, a one-way rule
  • The 11-field structure: 8 required (Skills, Framework Alignment, Issuer Accreditation, Evidence, Result, Criteria, Assessment, Achievement type) + 3 recommended (Rubric, Duration, Endorsement)
  • The technical stack: Implemented through OB 3.0 native capabilities (Alignment, ResultDescription, etc.) combined with the OBEE extensions (Issuer Accreditation, Assessment)
  • A clear taxonomy: Application (applied) and Knowledge (assessed) credentials are cleanly separated from Recognition Badges
  • Institutionalization underway: Framework first unveiled March 2024 → expanded version finalized May 12, 2026; 34 signatories; certification process in development

Selection Guide

Badge and certificate issuers → Start with the 11-field gap analysis
Rationale: Skills, Result, and Criteria are often already covered by existing flows, letting investment focus on the fields needing extension (Alignment, Accreditation, Assessment)
Platform and LMS vendors → OB 3.0 certification plus OBEE extension support
Rationale: Committed organizations must require certification of partners, so extension support is likely to become a partnership and procurement requirement
Employers and receiving institutions → Use Achievement type and Evidence as screening filters
Rationale: The Application/Knowledge type plus attached evidence quickly reveals a credential's substance

Overall Market Sentiment: Positive (a practice-oriented quality bar tied directly to the technical standard, broadly endorsed by big tech and education institutions; once the certification process is complete, it has the potential to become the market's de facto quality benchmark)

Key Terminology

TrustEd Credential - A name permitted only for digital credentials that satisfy all required metadata fields defined by the 1EdTech Coalition
OBEE (Open Badges Extensions for Education) - A family of education-specific extensions adding issuer accreditation and assessment information to badges; started by university systems, stewarded by 1EdTech
Issuer Accreditation Extension - The OBEE extension embedding information about the body (or bodies) that accredits the badge issuer — expressing "who vouches for the issuer's authority"
Assessment Extension - The OBEE extension describing the assessment(s) — description, method, date — required to earn the badge
Alignment class - A native Open Badges 3.0 capability linking a credential to external skill and competency frameworks such as CASE or CTDL
ResultDescription - The OB 3.0 native capability defining result types, ranges, and rubric levels; the implementation vehicle for the recommended Rubric field
CASE (Competencies and Academic Standards Exchange) - The 1EdTech standard for exchanging learning standards and competency definitions in machine-readable form; a primary target for Framework Alignment
Recognition Badge - A badge without formal assessment, such as event attendance or membership; outside the scope of TrustEd requirements
