Key Terminology

Text-to-Video — A technology where AI automatically generates video from text prompts. Representative examples include Sora and Veo
AI Avatar — A virtual person generated by AI that produces video of a lifelike figure speaking with natural facial expressions and lip-sync from text input. Used by platforms such as Synthesia and HeyGen
SCORM (Sharable Content Object Reference Model) — An international standard that ensures e-learning content is compatible with LMS (Learning Management Systems). Essential when deploying AI-generated videos to an LMS
VRAM (Video RAM) — The video memory of a GPU. Sufficient VRAM is required to run open-source video generation models locally (e.g., 8GB, 12GB, 24GB)
Quantization — A technique that reduces memory usage and computational load by lowering the precision of AI model operations. Enables large models to run on smaller GPUs
Native Audio Generation — The capability to simultaneously generate dialogue, sound effects, and ambient audio alongside video without a separate speech synthesis step. Supported by Veo 3 and Kling 2.6
Diffusion Model — An AI technique that progressively generates images or video from noise. Most current video generation models are built on this approach
Microlearning — A learning methodology built around short content units of 3–5 minutes. AI video generation makes mass production of microlearning videos significantly more feasible
